# Findings, run 2026-10-27

Every figure was verified at the source. Verified means the page says it, not that the study is sound.

## b1 Owner time lost to admin

Says: US owners of firms with five or fewer staff spend 22.4 percent of their time on admin plus legal and compliance, in the vendor's own survey [b1-01]. UK owners report 11 hours a week on admin or finance, in the vendor's own survey [b1-02]. US owners report one hour and 36 minutes a day lost to tasks they call unproductive, in a vendor-commissioned survey [b1-03].

Weak spots: all four sources are vendors; no independent figure was found. Units differ (share of time, hours a week, time a day) and must never be added or converted. "Unproductive" in [b1-03] is self-defined, not admin. [b1-04] is from 2015 and Australian.

Gap: what owners would give up to get the time back is answered only by [b1-04], a vendor figure. Nothing on willingness to pay.

## b2 Employee worry and acceptance

Says: in independent polling, 52 percent of US workers are worried about future AI use at work [b2-01], 66 percent of US adults expect data from AI evaluation of workers to be misused [b2-02], 40 percent of Australians trust AI at work [b2-07]. Vendor figures agree: 42 percent trust their employer to use AI for employees [b2-05]; 74 percent want legislation on training AI with employee data [b2-06].

Contradiction: 43 percent of office workers would probably or definitely let their company analyse their messages with AI, in the vendor's own survey [b2-03].

Weak spots: Pew sample sizes were not captured in this run. [b2-08] samples adults while its unit says workers, and its 36 is not the README's Pew "hopeful" figure. [b2-04] is UK and Microsoft-commissioned. [b2-07] gives no Australian subsample.

Gap: nothing on what makes employees accept a specific assistant, or whether refusing to evaluate people raises trust.

## b3 Knowledge lost at joining and leaving

Says: average onboarding takes 4.8 months [b3-01] (tech talent, 418 records, not small firms). Replacement recruiting costs 20 percent of base salary, a vendor figure with no stated sample [b3-02]. 84 percent call it a big loss when older staff retire without passing on knowledge, in the vendor's own survey fielded in 2021 [b3-03].

Gap: no small-company source, and nothing showing that a handover note or a first-days plan shortens anything.

## b4 Paper and photo documents

Says: 45 percent of SMB operations rely on paper, in the vendor's own survey, country not stated [b4-01]. Further vendor figures are Australian and old: [b4-02], [b4-03], [b4-04], [b4-08]. In informal Central American microbusinesses, 44 percent keep systematic records, mostly in notebooks [b4-10] (independent, 1,400 firms).

On automatic reading: OCR entry in intensive care reached 96.9% accuracy and cut 6.0 to 3.4 minutes per patient [b4-07] (peer reviewed, hospitals). A vendor benchmark says the best models struggled to exceed 95% on hand-filled forms [b4-09].

Weak spots: mostly vendor, Australian and old; [b4-05] and [b4-06] predate phone capture. No current figure for the target market.

Agreement: [b4-07] and [b4-09] fit the README limit: clean printed forms read well, handwriting is the open risk.

## b5 Competing tools

Says, each a vendor figure: ChatGPT Business 25 USD per user per month [b5-02], Lark Pro 12 USD per user [b5-05], Vellum Pro from 50 USD a month [b5-01], Cassidy free tier with 3 seats [b5-04]. An independent guide confirms the two-seat minimum for ChatGPT Business [b5-06]. 43 percent of businesses with 2 to 9 staff use AI, a vendor figure cited second hand [b5-03].

Source notes: one workspace product already offers role-based AI teammates scoped to user permissions, and business tiers of a general assistant reportedly do not train on data by default. Neither "role-based" nor "no training" is unique.

Gap: no source compares tools on refusing to evaluate people, code-checked handoffs or stateless chat. Rankings come from vendors ranking themselves.

## b6 Hackathon and Meta models

Says: a 10-day virtual event with a US $1 million prize pool, a vendor figure from a listing site [b6-01]; 150 USD API credit per participant, from a secondary report [b6-02]. Spark 1.3 Standard pricing [b6-03] and a 1,048,576-token context [b6-04] are vendor figures from Meta, whose pages also say Standard content is not used for training. The open-weights license needs a separate agreement above $20 million revenue over 12 months [b6-06]; Muse Spark Open has 816 billion total and 42 billion active parameters [b6-07].

Gap: no judging criteria captured; the credit is not confirmed on a Meta page.

## What this does not show

The README must not claim:

1. That the product saves owners time; nothing here measures it.
2. A combined or converted admin-time figure.
3. That employees will adopt the assistant, or that refusing evaluation skills raises trust.
4. That competitors evaluate, monitor or train on business data.
5. That it is cheaper than seat-priced tools; per-call cost and per-seat price are not comparable.
6. That photo reading works on handwriting or phone photos.
7. A current paper-use rate for small firms in any market.
8. That handovers or first-days plans shorten onboarding.
9. Judging criteria or the API credit, from this run.
10. README Impact rows not found again here (Pew "watched", Cisco, Gallup, Pew sample size) unless their linked pages are rechecked.
